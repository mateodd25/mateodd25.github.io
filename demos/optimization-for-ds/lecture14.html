The one-step estimate and telescoping argument

‖wj+1 − w⋆‖² ≤ ‖wj − w⋆‖² − 2ηj(f(wj) − f⋆) + ηj²G².

Sum from j = 0 to k − 1. The intermediate squared distances cancel. Dropping −‖wk−w⋆‖², dividing by 2Σηj, and applying convexity gives the displayed bound. At k = 0 the average is undefined.

2 What does the proximal map minimize?

Move the input v or change η. The quadratic term anchors the solution near v, while the absolute-value term pulls it toward zero.

h(z) = |z|,   proxηh(v) = arg minz { |z| + (z − v)²/(2η) } = sign(v) max{|v| − η, 0}.

Optimality condition from the notes

w⁺ = proxηh(v) ⇔ (v − w⁺)/η ∈ ∂h(w⁺).

For h(z) = |z|, the subdifferential at zero is [−1, 1]. The proximal point equals zero exactly when v/η belongs to this interval. At a positive proximal point the selected subgradient is 1; at a negative point it is −1.
